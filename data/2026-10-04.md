It defines 23 metrics across five categories and uses an LLM-as-judge pipeline calibrated against human coders. <details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing evaluations of LLM-integrated HEMS mainly focus on single-turn or single-task settings and prioritize response accuracy. These approaches do not adequately assess the sustained, context-aware, interactive behavior required in realistic home energy management, where systems must account for occupants’ backgrounds, preferences, contextual information, and control-related needs. The study therefore seeks a reproducible and scalable evaluation methodology for multi-turn HEMS interactions.

Method: The authors develop a Goal-Question-Metric-based evaluation framework with 23 metrics grouped into five dimensions: task performance, factual accuracy, interaction quality, control capability, and system efficiency. They implement an LLM-as-judge scoring pipeline and validate its reliability against three trained human coders through iterative rubric calibration. The framework is applied to 970 generated dialogues covering 16 scenarios and five personas, evaluated across four conversational HEMS configurations representing increasing sophistication—from a vanilla LLM supplied with raw energy data to a multi-agent HEMS. Agreement is assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC), while metrics with near-zero human-score variance are reported using mean absolute error.

Result: Twelve of the fifteen LLM-scored metrics achieved strong agreement with human judgments (ICC ≥ 0.73), including three metrics with perfect agreement after rubric calibration. The remaining three metrics showed near-zero variance in human scores and were assessed using mean absolute error values ranging from 0.04 to 0.28. Across the 970 dialogues, the framework differentiated the four HEMS configurations across multiple dimensions and exposed distinct strengths and weaknesses.

Conclusion: The study demonstrates that a calibrated LLM-as-judge framework can provide scalable and largely reliable evaluation of conversational HEMS beyond response correctness alone. Its multi-dimensional, multi-turn design supports comprehensive assessment of sustained, context-aware performance and offers a reproducible basis for comparing HEMS architectures. The main contribution is methodological: a validated evaluation framework rather than a new HEMS control algorithm.
