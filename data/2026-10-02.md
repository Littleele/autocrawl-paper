*Wooyoung Jung*

Main category: cs.HC

TL;DR: The paper proposes and validates a comprehensive, multi-dimensional framework for evaluating LLM-integrated home energy management systems (HEMS) in realistic multi-turn interactions. It defines 23 metrics across five categories and uses a calibrated LLM-as-judge pipeline, validated against human coders, to compare four HEMS configurations over 970 dialogues.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing evaluations of LLM-integrated HEMS mainly use single-turn or single-task tests and emphasize response accuracy. They do not adequately measure sustained dialogue behavior, contextual adaptation, user interaction quality, control capability, or efficiency. The paper addresses the need for a reproducible and scalable framework that reflects real-world multi-turn HEMS use.

Method: The authors develop a Goal-Question-Metric-based evaluation framework with five dimensions: task performance, factual accuracy, interaction quality, control capability, and system efficiency. The framework contains 23 multi-turn metrics. An LLM-as-judge pipeline performs automated scoring, with rubrics iteratively calibrated against three trained human coders. The framework is applied to 970 generated dialogues covering 16 scenarios and five personas, and compares four HEMS configurations that range from a vanilla LLM using raw energy data to a multi-agent HEMS.

Result: Twelve of fifteen LLM-scored metrics achieved strong agreement with human coders (ICC ≥ 0.73), including three with perfect agreement. The other three metrics showed near-zero variance in human ratings and were therefore evaluated using mean absolute error, ranging from 0.04 to 0.28. Across the 970 dialogues, the framework differentiated the four HEMS configurations across multiple dimensions and exposed distinct strengths and weaknesses.

Conclusion: The study demonstrates that a calibrated LLM-as-judge framework can provide scalable and reasonably reliable evaluation of conversational HEMS. Its main contribution is a reproducible, multi-dimensional methodology for assessing sustained, context-aware performance rather than isolated response accuracy. The framework also offers a basis for systematic comparison and future improvement of LLM-integrated HEMS.
