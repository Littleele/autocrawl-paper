### [1] [TypedInk: Knowing When Not To Draw](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13027)
*Weiwei Yang*

Main category: cs.HC

TL;DR: TypedInk is an evidence-aware scientific figure generator that first classifies the requested figure as a diagram, plot, or plate, then dispatches only when the available source satisfies that figure type’s evidence requirements. It renders supported cases and refuses or labels under-specified cases instead of fabricating quantitative results. On the reported benchmark, it preserves observed table values, refuses hidden-table plots, and identifies intent-only outputs as illustrative, avoiding execution failures and integrity violations caused by unconditional drawing.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Most scientific figure generators assume complete inputs such as a finished methods section or an observed data table. In practice, source material may be incomplete, ranging from outlines and draft text to one-sentence intents. Generating plots without measured data risks hallucinating scientific results, while selecting an inappropriate renderer can also produce outputs that misrepresent the evidence. The paper therefore addresses the need for abstention, evidence contracts, and reliable figure-type routing under incomplete source conditions.

Method: TypedInk infers a figure type from diagram, plot, and plate using the available source material, then dispatches to a renderer whose evidence contract matches that type. The system refuses dispatch when the contract cannot be satisfied, marks intent-only outputs as illustrative, and uses a code path for plots when a table is available. Evaluation uses TypedFigBench with six gold plots and five views per item (n=30), plus a diagnostic type-router ablation over 16 illustrations and five views each (n=80). Comparisons include an always-draw coder and a chat-model planner; evaluation also reports table-token recovery, refusal behavior, execution failures, type accuracy, integrity failures, diagram-label recall, and ratings from two vision-language raters.

Result: When tables were observed, TypedInk’s code path reproduced all gold table tokens (12/12); it refused all 12 hidden-table views and marked all six intent-only views as illustrative, with no execution failures. The always-draw baseline produced charts for all 30 views, including the 18 lacking tables, but recovered zero gold numbers in those cases; its outputs were judged submittable 17/36 times by two vision-language raters, whereas TypedInk’s refusals were judged submittable 0/36 times. In routing ablations, outlines alone achieved only 0.31 type accuracy and sent every gold plot to an integrity-violating route. A chat-model planner improved accuracy to 0.85 but still produced integrity failures for 25% of outlines. Diagram-label recall was 0.47 for the full record and 0.19 for outlines. Gold plates were typed at dispatch with an oracle integrity-failure rate of 0.00 and rendered as still images.

Conclusion: The results support evidence-aware abstention as a useful safeguard for scientific figure generation: refusing to draw when required evidence is absent is preferable to producing plausible but unsupported quantitative graphics. TypedInk demonstrates strong integrity behavior on the reported cases, especially for table-backed plots and under-specified inputs. However, figure-type inference remains imperfect for outlines, diagram-label recall is limited, and the evaluation relies on only two vision-language raters rather than human judges; broader benchmarks and human replication are needed.

Abstract: Can a scientific figure generator know when it does not have enough evidence to draw? Complete-source illustrators map a finished methodology section to a schematic, or a table to plotting code, and are never asked this question because their input is assumed complete. The research record is rarely that complete: an outline, a draft paragraph, a table, or a one-sentence intent, in any combination, and painting measured numbers with an image model when the table is missing is a hallucinated result. TypedInk infers a figure type from {diagram, plot, plate} from a possibly incomplete source and dispatches to the renderer that can honor that type's evidence contract, refusing dispatch when the contract cannot be met. On the six gold plots in TypedFigBench (5 views, n=30), TypedInk's code path inks every gold table token when a table is observed (12/12), refuses the 12 hidden-table views instead of inventing numbers, and marks 6 intent-only views illustrative, with no execution failures. An always-draw coder with no such gate inks 30/30 charts, including all 18 with no table, and recovers zero gold numbers on any of them; two vision-language raters (not humans; human replication is future work) still judge those always-draw charts submittable 17/36 times, against TypedInk's refusal 0/36 times. A diagnostic field ablation of the type router (16 illustrations, 5 views each, n=80) locates where this failure would originate if unchecked: an outline alone yields type accuracy 0.31 and sends every gold plot into an integrity violation, and a chat-model planner narrows but does not close the gap (overall accuracy 0.85, still integrity-failing on 25% of outlines). Diagram-label recall is 0.47 on the full record and 0.19 on an outline. Gold plates are typed at dispatch (oracle integrity-fail 0.00) and rendered as stills.
